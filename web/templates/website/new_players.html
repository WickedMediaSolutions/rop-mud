New Players Guide

Welcome to Rites of Passage! Never played a MUD before? No problem. This guide will walk you through everything you need to know to get from your first connection to your first adventure.

What Is a MUD?

MUD stands for Multi-User Dungeon. Think of it as an online multiplayer RPG where the graphics are driven entirely by your imagination, described through rich text. You type commands, and the world responds. There are no pictures, no clicks — only words, choices, and consequences.

If you've played games like World of Warcraft, Skyrim, or Baldur's Gate, you'll find many familiar concepts: classes, stats, combat, quests, loot, and exploration. The key difference is that everything happens through text.

"You are in a torch-lit chamber deep beneath the fortress. The air is thick with the smell of damp stone and old blood. A narrow passage leads north. Faint chanting echoes from somewhere below. In the corner, a chest glints in the flickering light."

Step-by-Step: Your First Session

1. Open the Web Client

Click Play Now in the navigation bar to open the browser-based client. It's split into two parts: the large output pane where the game world appears, and a text input bar at the bottom where you type commands.

2. The Login Screen

When you first connect, you'll see an ornate ASCII art screen and a prompt. This is where you create your account:

create MyName MySecretPassword

Replace MyName with the username you want, and MySecretPassword with a real password. Remember these — you'll need them every time you play.

Next time you connect, use:

connect MyName MySecretPassword

3. Character Creation

After logging in, you'll enter the character creation menu. Here you can:

  1. Create a new character — follow the prompts to choose your race, class, and character name.
  2. Pick a race — this determines your faction (Good or Evil), starting stats, and a special passive ability. Read the descriptions carefully!
  3. Pick a class — this is your profession. Warriors are straightforward for new players; Mages offer depth if you enjoy complexity.
  4. Name your character — pick a name that fits the dark fantasy setting.

Once created, you'll automatically enter the game world at your faction's starting city.

Understanding the Screen

When you first enter the world, here's what you'll see (and what it means):

SectionWhat It Tells You
Room NameThe title of your current location, e.g., "Aethelgard Grand Sanctum".
Room DescriptionA paragraph describing what you see, hear, and smell.
CharactersAny players or NPCs standing in the room with you.
ItemsObjects on the floor that you can pick up.
ExitsThe directions you can travel from this room (e.g., [north, east, in]).

Pro tip: Type look (or just l) at any time to see the room description again.

Your First Five Commands

Here are the five most important commands to learn right now. Try each one:

#CommandTry It To…
1look or lSee the room description again.
2n, s, e, wMove around (north, south, east, west).
3helpOpen the full help system.
4say helloSpeak to other players in the room.
5stats or scView your character stats (HP, mana, attributes).

Choosing Your Faction: Good vs. Evil

☀️

Good — Aethelgard

The forces of Light gather in the ancient city of Aethelgard. As a Good-aligned character you'll start in the Grand Sanctum. Races: Human, High Elf, Wood Elf, Mountain Dwarf, Stout Halfling, Gnome, Centaur, Pixie.

💀

Evil — Gorgoroth

The legions of Darkness muster in the volcanic citadel of Gorgoroth. As an Evil-aligned character you'll start in the Blood Forge. Races: Orc, Dark Elf, Undead, Goblin, Minotaur, Lizardfolk, Ogre, Demonkin.

Your faction choice determines who your allies are, where you begin your journey, and the tone of your starting experience. Both sides have equal content and opportunities.

Common Beginner Mistakes (and How to Avoid Them)

❌ Not Typing look Often

The room description is your eyes. If you're lost or confused, type l. It's a completely free action and often contains clues about exits, hidden items, or NPCs to talk to.

❌ Wandering Into High-Level Zones

The world is open, but not all areas are safe for a new character. If a room description sounds dangerous ("the air reeks of death", "massive footprints scar the floor"), it probably is. Use consider before attacking anything.

❌ Ignoring Exits

Always check which directions are listed. You might miss secret doors, climbable surfaces, or portals. Type exits for a clean list of available exits.

❌ Not Using the Help System

help shows the main help menu. help combat, help magic, help communication — there are dozens of detailed help files. Use them!

❌ Playing Alone

MUDs are social games. Don't be shy — say hello to other players. Ask questions. The community is the best resource for learning the ropes and finding adventure partners.

FAQ

Do I lose my character when I log out?

No! Your character is saved permanently and will be right where you left them.

Can I have more than one character?

Yes. You can create multiple characters on one account from the character menu.

Is the game free to play?

Yes. Rites of Passage is completely free.

What if I get stuck?

Use help, ask other players with tell or say, or contact a game admin.

Can I switch factions later?

No. Your faction choice is permanent — choose wisely. However, you can always create a new character on the other side.
